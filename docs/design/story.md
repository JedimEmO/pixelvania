# Story

What happens in the game, who the player meets, and what they say.
The [vision](vision.md) sets the theme and the rules: the story lives in the rooms,
in a few lines of dialogue and in what the guests are doing, and it never preaches.
This page holds the vertical slice's story in full and the shape of the rest;
[the Arrival](arrival.md) has the opening's rooms and words.

## The premise

You wake on a chaise in a vestibule, late, alone, still dressed for the evening.
The front doors behind you are shut.
Music comes through the wall. On the cushion beside you is a card:
*You are expected. Do come through. — H.*

Nobody tells you who you are, how long you slept, or how long the party has been going.
The guests you meet have been at it so long they no longer remember either,
and none of them finds that strange.

The Host, H., is never seen in the slice.
Their cards are everywhere, in the same gracious hand, and every one asks you to stay.

## How it is told

Guests who talk. Some guests are not hostile: each is stuck in a loop, doing one thing forever.
Press up beside one to talk. Each has a few lines, and the lines change as you find abilities,
so going back to someone is rewarded.

The Host's cards. A small glowing card in a room, read with up. One or two lines each.
They read as hospitality, and later as something closer to a warning.

Rooms that are about something. Every room has a purpose and the props to show it:
the card room has a game abandoned mid-hand, the cloakroom has coats nobody came back for,
the bandstand has instruments set down mid-song. Hostile guests are doing the room's thing too:
the toaster in the card room toasts a winning hand, the spinner on the dance floor never stopped.

Staged moments. A few scenes take the controls away for a breath:
waking up, the first sight of the ballroom, the Dancer's entrance and her fall.

## The guests who talk

Each line is shown in a box at the bottom of the screen, a line at a time.
Conditions are the abilities found; the last matching set is the one shown.

### The attendant, in the coat check

A small, neat person behind the counter, handing coats to nobody.

At first:
- "Ticket? No? Nobody keeps their ticket."
- "I have hung every coat in this house. Not one has been asked for back."
- "Go on through. They are all through there."

With the Lunge:
- "In a hurry. Everyone is, the first night."
- "It passes."

With the Lure:
- "You have the look of someone who has seen the chandeliers up close."
- "Mind the moths. They never stop either."

### The partner, in the green room

The Dancer's partner, slumped at a mirror, shoes off, unable to stand any more.

At first:
- "You're going to see her. Of course you are."
- "I kept up with her for... I don't know. A long time. Then I couldn't."
- "She never notices when you stop. She just finds someone new."

With the Plunge:
- "The music stopped. I heard it stop."
- "I thought I would be glad."

### The bandleader, on the bandstand

Still conducting an orchestra that went home long ago.

At first:
- "One more, she said. One more and then we'll all go to bed."
- "That was the last request I took."
- "Listen. Can you hear them? I can always hear them."

With the Plunge:
- "The tempo's gone. Who's keeping time?"

### The wallflower, on the balcony

Has been about to ask someone to dance for as long as the party has run.

At first:
- "I'm going to ask her. Tonight. In a moment."
- "Once I have had a little air."

With the Swoon:
- "It goes to your head, doesn't it. The height. All of it."

With the Plunge:
- "She isn't dancing any more? Then I've missed it."
- "Well. There will be another."

### The card player, in the card room

Deals a hand to four empty chairs, and wins every time.

At first:
- "Sit, sit. Stakes are low. Everything is low, by now."
- "You can bank what you win, at the lounges. Or drink it. Most drink it."
- "Drink it and the room comes alive again. For a while. Less each time."

With the Lure:
- "I used to play for the chandeliers. Imagine. Climbing for them."

## The Host's cards

- Vestibule, on the chaise: *You are expected. Do come through. — H.*
- Coat check: *Leave whatever you came in with. You will not need it. — H.*
- Antechamber: *You found your way in. They always do. — H.*
- Cloakroom: *Nobody has come back for these. Nobody will. — H.*
- Champagne cellar: *Take what you like. There is always more. — H.*
- Mirrored corridor: *Look how well you are doing. — H.*
- Green room: *She has been waiting for a partner who can keep up. Can you? — H.*
- Cellar, at the end of the slice: *Still here? How lovely. The night is young. — H.*

## Staged moments

Waking. A new game opens on black. The music is muffled, as through a wall.
The picture fades up slowly on the vestibule, the player lying on the chaise,
and the first card glows beside them. The controls come back when the player stands.

The ballroom. The first time the player steps through its door, the music opens up,
from muffled to full, and the camera holds a moment on the hall.

The Dancer's entrance. On the stage she is dancing alone. When the player comes near
she stops, turns, and the boss music starts. Her lines, one per phase:
- The waltz: "There you are. Dance with me."
- The frenzy: "Faster. Faster!"
- Burning out: "Don't let it stop. Don't let it stop."
- Her fall: "Was it lovely? Tell me it was lovely."

Then the floor gives way.

The cellar. Silence after the music. One card glows on the floor: the Host's last line of the slice.
The slice ends there, with the words *The night goes on.*

## Beyond the slice

The other areas follow the same pattern: one guest per area who talks about the area's boss,
cards from the Host that grow less gracious, and a boss who is one answer to the question.
[Progression](progression.md) has the order. The two endings are in the [vision](vision.md).
